🔄 Unit Conversions

Master unit conversions — the #1 skill that costs students points on the AP exam. Learn the method, study the references, and practice until it's automatic.

⚠️ Why Conversions Are Critical on the AP Exam

Every AP Physics 1 problem requires SI base units. If you plug in mph instead of m/s, or grams instead of kg, your answer will be wrong — even if your physics is perfect. AP graders deduct points for wrong units OR missing units.

Pro tip from Princeton Review: ALWAYS convert to SI base units (meters, kilograms, seconds) BEFORE plugging into any equation. Convert once at the start, then never worry about units again!

🎯 The Dimensional Analysis Method (Factor-Label Method)

This is THE only method you need. It works for ANY conversion, no matter how complex. The trick: multiply by fractions equal to 1.

📋 The 4-Step Process:
  1. Write down what you have with units (e.g., 60 mph)
  2. Multiply by a conversion fraction where the unit you want to cancel is on the BOTTOM and the unit you want is on the TOP (e.g., × 1609 m / 1 mi)
  3. Cancel matching units (cross out matching units like fractions)
  4. Multiply numbers, write units that didn't cancel
💡 Worked Example: Convert 60 mph to m/s
Step 1: 60 mi/hr
Step 2: 60 mi/hr × (1609 m / 1 mi) × (1 hr / 3600 s)
Step 3: Cancel mi and hr (top mi cancels bottom mi, bottom hr cancels top hr)
Step 4: = (60 × 1609) / 3600 = 26.8 m/s
✅ Pro Tips That Save Points
  • • Always convert FIRST, before doing any physics
  • • Write units in EVERY step — they're your error-detector
  • • If your final units are weird (like kg·m²·s⁻³), you made a mistake
  • • Memorize the SI base units: meters, kilograms, seconds, Newtons, Joules, Watts
  • • Use scientific notation for very large/small numbers (avoids errors)
❌ Common Mistakes That Lose Points
  • • Forgetting to convert grams → kilograms (off by 1000!)
  • • Using cm instead of m in formulas (off by 100!)
  • • Forgetting that Hz = 1/s and Pa = N/m²
  • • Confusing kg with N (mass vs weight)
  • • Squaring units incorrectly (m² ≠ 100 cm², it's 10,000 cm²!)
  • • Forgetting units in the final answer (instant point loss on FRQ)
⚠️ The Squared/Cubed Units Trap

When converting squared or cubed units, you must square or cube the conversion factor!

WRONG: 1 m² = 100 cm² ❌ (this would be just multiplying by 100)
RIGHT: 1 m² = (100 cm)² = 10,000 cm² ✅
WRONG: 1 m³ = 1000 cm³ ❌
RIGHT: 1 m³ = (100 cm)³ = 1,000,000 cm³ ✅
